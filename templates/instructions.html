⚄ Dice Wars
Campaign Instructions Behind the Scenes
The Objective

Conquer the map

Dice Wars is a territory-control game. The map is split between you (wax-seal red) and the AI general (steel blue). On your turn, attack adjacent enemy territories using dice. Win enough battles and control the whole map — or simply hold more territory than the AI when the campaign settles.

Combat

How an attack works

Every territory holds 1–8 dice. To attack, click one of your territories with 2+ dice, then click an adjacent enemy territory. All of your attacker's dice are rolled against all of the defender's dice — highest total wins.

If you win: the defender's territory becomes yours. All but one of your attacking dice move in to occupy it. If you lose: your attacking territory is left with just 1 die, and the defender is untouched. Either way, only a territory with 2+ dice can attack, so a losing attack takes you out of action there until you reinforce.

Reinforcements

Rebuilding your line

When you're done attacking for the turn, click End Attack Phase. You receive one reinforcement die for every territory in your largest connected group — so keeping your holdings linked up matters as much as their number. Click your own territories to place dice one at a time (max 8 per territory), or use Auto-place rest to let the game distribute what's left to your most exposed borders.

The Opponent

You're not playing a script

The AI general is a small pipeline of three cooperating agents — one sets the turn's overall strategy, one executes attacks, one places reinforcements. Watch the Behind the Scenes — Live panel during play to see its reasoning in real time, or visit the Behind the Scenes page for the full architecture.

Maps

Three theaters

Skirmish Isle is small and fast — good for learning the flow. Twin Peninsulas is a mid-sized map with natural choke points. Grand Continent is a sprawling 40-territory campaign for longer games. All three are generated as organic landmasses, not a grid of nodes, so borders and choke points actually matter.
